# WebNRD content, SEO & AEO plan

**Working draft for Nate Martinez · Reviewed November 24, 2026**

## What to change first

The main portfolio already has a voice worth keeping: direct, practical, and personal. Lines like “I design the site. Then I build it.” and “Send me the messy version” sound like a real person explaining how he works. The service introductions, service cards, location pages, and several FAQ answers switch into third person or refer to “the practice.” The result feels less consistent than the portfolio itself. [1]

The draft below keeps the person behind the work visible. It gives visitors straightforward answers while still naming the services, location, and projects people may search for. It also avoids pretending that the portfolio examples are local clients or that any result is guaranteed.

**Before publishing:** Confirm the offers, cities, meeting options, social media scope, and project credits in the final checklist. The existing website is the source for those details, but this draft is not a substitute for your approval. This is proposed content; nothing on the live site has been changed.

## Voice guide

- Write as **I** when describing your work and **you** when talking to the visitor. Use **we** for the parts you and the client do together.
- Introduce your name naturally: “I’m Nate Martinez,” not “Nate Martinez is…” in the body copy. A name in a page title, logo, or factual structured-data field is still useful and does not need to be forced into first person.
- Sound capable without sounding like an agency: say “I’ll map the pages” instead of “our expert team delivers solutions.” Do not imply you work alone on every project if other contributors were involved.
- Lead with the answer. Add just enough detail to help a visitor decide what to do next.
- Keep your personality: plain language, short sentences, a little warmth, and no unnecessary marketing jargon. Keep “Send me the messy version.”
- Be specific about the work you did. Avoid invented metrics, testimonials, clients, local offices, rankings, or promises. Do not describe a website example as proof of SEO or social media results unless you can substantiate that work.

# SEO and AEO implementation plan

## 1. Make the site sound like one person

Replace the third-person introductions, service cards, and FAQ answers with approved first-person versions above. Keep distinctive lines that already work. Review the site’s navigation, social-sharing descriptions, image alt text, and any reusable content files for the same voice. Descriptive image alt text should describe the image and context; it does not need to be first person.

Update the visible FAQ answers and their matching JSON-LD together if the FAQ markup is retained. The homepage currently contains both. Also update applicable WebSite, Person, ProfessionalService, and Service descriptions so they accurately reflect the revised public content. Preserve structured identity fields such as your actual name, service type, and URL rather than trying to write every schema value as “I.” [1] [5]

## 2. Align each page with a real visitor need

Use these as **candidate topics**, not confirmed keyword-volume or ranking data:

- **Homepage:** Who you are, what you do, where you are based, and why someone might work with you. Candidate phrase: “web designer and developer in Lake Elsinore.”
- **Web development:** The process, what you build, what happens at launch, and examples. Candidate phrase: “web design and development in Lake Elsinore.”
- **Local SEO:** Profile ownership, the work involved, realistic expectations, and a relevant example. Candidate phrase: “local SEO in Lake Elsinore.”
- **Social media:** Platforms, organic versus paid work, approvals, and how posts connect to the site. Candidate phrase: “social media management in Lake Elsinore.”
- **Areas:** Where you work and how someone in each area can engage you. Don’t create another page just to swap in a different city name.
- **Project pages:** The project challenge, your role, your decision-making, and evidence. Link back to the relevant service without presenting a design project as proof of marketing results.

Check Search Console’s actual queries, impressions, clicks, and pages before prioritizing specific terms. If relevant data is sparse, use the candidate phrases as hypotheses and refine them as the site gains data.

## 3. Improve answers without chasing an “AI hack”

The answer-first format is useful because it helps people understand your offer—not because short answers or FAQ schema guarantee an AI citation. Google says the same foundational SEO practices apply to AI search, with emphasis on original, useful content and a clear technical structure. There is no required AI-specific schema, magic word count, or need for an llms.txt file for Google Search. [5]

Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in May 2026. Keep or remove FAQPage markup based on maintainability and accuracy; don’t expect it to produce a Google FAQ rich result. The visible questions still have value when they answer genuine customer concerns. [6]

Prioritize distinctive content you can substantiate: for example, how you organized MyOrthodontist’s location information or how you approach a technical product hierarchy. A specific account of your own decisions is more valuable than generic lists of SEO tips.

## 4. Review location pages carefully

Several city pages follow a similar structure and discuss the same three services. That does not, by itself, prove a policy violation. It does mean each page should earn its place with genuinely useful, accurate information. Google’s doorway policy warns about near-duplicate pages created for similar city queries that funnel visitors to the same destination. [2]

For each city page, ask: Does it help someone in that city make a decision that the main service-area page does not already answer? Can you add first-hand information, verified local work, a meaningful engagement detail, or a useful local question? If not, consider consolidating it into the areas page rather than creating more versions. Before consolidating, check performance and inbound links, then map appropriate 301 redirects and update internal links and sitemap entries.

## 5. Check technical consistency and crawler choices

- The homepage returned successfully, declares `index, follow`, and has a canonical URL. The site’s robots file points to `sitemap-index.xml`, which returned a sitemap index. These are observations, not proof that every page is indexed or ranking. [1] [7]
- In Google Search Console, inspect the homepage, main services, and one or two city and project pages. Confirm indexing, canonical selection, and any access issues. Submit or confirm the sitemap index and request recrawling after material changes where appropriate. Separately, test the mobile experience and performance with real devices and PageSpeed Insights.
- Check the Search Console setting for inclusion in Google Search’s generative AI features. Also confirm that the linked Google Maps profile belongs to this business and that its real-world name and the visible site and structured data are consistent. Remove an incorrect profile link instead of inventing an address or business identity. [10]
- Check that each page has an appropriate title, meta description, one clear main heading, readable text links, and visible answers that agree with any structured data. Validate changes with Google’s relevant testing tools.
- The current robots file blocks **GPTBot** and **ClaudeBot**, and has a general allow rule. That is not automatically an AI-search problem: OpenAI and Anthropic document separate search and training crawlers. The file does not explicitly name **OAI-SearchBot** or **Claude-SearchBot**. Review the provider documentation and your Cloudflare/WAF settings before changing anything; server-side bot controls can be separate from robots.txt. Choose access based on your preferences rather than unblocking training crawlers by default. [7] [8] [9]
- Do not assume any AI system has indexed or cited the site. This review did not test ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, or Grok visibility.

## 6. Publish in a sensible order

**First pass:** Confirm your offers and rewrite the homepage, service introductions, service cards, and homepage/service FAQs. Update the matching metadata and structured descriptions at the same time.

**Second pass:** Improve one strong case study with a clear challenge, your role, a concrete design decision, and evidence. Then review the six area pages for distinct value and update or consolidate them thoughtfully.

**Third pass:** Add useful articles only where you have a perspective or example worth sharing. Three good starting ideas:

1. How I decide what belongs on a website before designing it.
2. What a multi-location practice website needs to make finding an office easier.
3. How I organize a technical product website for people who already know what they need.

Each article should link naturally to the most relevant service or case study and include your actual process, examples, or screenshots where you have permission. These topics are suggestions, not claims of established search demand.

## 7. Measure what matters

Before changing the site, record a baseline in Search Console: indexed status for the main pages, search impressions, clicks, queries, and top pages. Track qualified contact-form submissions, email clicks, and phone clicks if analytics is configured. Search Console does not show all leads or all activity in every AI product.

Compare a meaningful period before and after the changes, ideally with similar seasonal conditions. Annotate publication dates and avoid attributing every change to the rewrite. If the Generative AI performance report is available in your Search Console property, use it alongside the regular Search performance report; availability and reporting may vary. [5]

For a lightweight AI visibility check, repeat a small set of relevant prompts manually at a consistent interval and record the date, tool, prompt, whether the response mentioned or linked to your site, and any citation. Example prompts: “Who designs websites for businesses near Lake Elsinore?” and “How do I choose a web designer for a multi-location practice?” Answers vary by product, location, account, prompt, and time; this is directional monitoring, not a ranking or market-share score.
